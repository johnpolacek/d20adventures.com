## GM Notes

The elves are gone and take no part. Silas and Reinhard do not know anyone is outside, unless Poppen or Thalbern has already shown himself or come aboard. One who has stays where he was as the scene opens: the door shuts with him inside the saloon or out on deck, and the two men know he is there and mean to be rid of Lyra all the same. The saloon's port windows are lit, unglazed and face the bank, their shutters propped open, so a watcher on the bank can see the fight and shoot in through them, with the sill as half cover. Each is wide enough for a person to climb through, which takes all of one's movement and needs no check. The thicket and the bank below the saloon's open windows both have a line in through them. The tug's stern deck does not: the solid aft cabin stands between it and the saloon, so a watcher who climbs aboard at the stern sees and hits nothing inside until he comes forward to the saloon door.

Silas and Reinhard want no witness and no body on their boat. They mean to overpower Lyra, throw her over the side into the river and cast off with the crate for Kordavos. This is the most likely way the fight ends, and it should take about three rounds. Reinhard closes and takes hold of her, drags her out on deck and puts her over the rail. Then Silas cuts the mooring lines and the current takes the tug downstream into the mist. Once she is in the water the two men do not stay to fight anyone on the bank. They cast off at once.

Silas cuts the lines only once Lyra is in the river, never while she is still aboard. Until then the tug lies tied fast to the pier and does not drift. Neither man can be talked or bargained out of it: they have their orders. A threat from outside, a shout, an arrow or a claim that the watch is coming, makes them hurry to put her over the side, not spare her. If Lyra is down, Reinhard carries her out and over the rail. If Reinhard is down, Silas takes over his work: he drags Lyra to the rail or a river-side window and puts her over, and never holds her as a shield or bargains with her life. A standoff does not hold: whoever has hold of Lyra presses for the river every turn. With the door held against him, he heaves her out through one of the saloon's open windows on the river side, which takes his action and needs no check against a downed or grappled Lyra. A hero who bars the doorway must be shoved aside first, with the Shove option of Reinhard's Unarmed Strike: the hero makes a Strength or Dexterity saving throw, their choice, against Reinhard's Shove DC, and on a failure is pushed 5 feet aside. A spell works as it says: one Charmed by her will not harm her, but the other still means to put her over the side. Charm Person makes a man friendly to her, not her servant: he will not lay hands on her or help throw her over, but he does not fight his partner for her. He may argue for her, or stand aside. A blow from his partner does not end the charm, only harm from Lyra or her allies does.

Give the heroes fair rolls to stop it, but do not drag the fight out. Lyra going overboard is not a defeat to avoid at any cost. It is how the story most often goes on.

The fight ends another way only if the heroes plainly win it. If Silas and Reinhard are both down, bound or driven off the boat, the crate is the heroes'. If Lyra gets off the boat by her own doing and loses them, she has escaped.

Poppen and Thalbern hide in different places and do not know of each other. Poppen is in the bushes at the treeline above the gangway. Thalbern is in a thicket on the bank below the stern. Each learns of the other only by passing a check. Ask for a Perception check, DC 15, when one of them searches or watches the bank or the woods, or looks for where an arrow, a voice or a noise came from. Use DC 10 once the other has acted in the open, with a shot, a shout or a step onto the pier. With no check or a failed one, he notices nothing of the other, and the narration shows nothing passing between them. A passed check shows a stranger with no name: a shape in the leaves, a bow, a small figure.
