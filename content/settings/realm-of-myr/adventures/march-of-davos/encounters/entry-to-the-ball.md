## Intro

In formal finery, their faces hidden behind the ornate masks of the masquerade, the adventurers approach the Asterian Quarter. The Blackthorn Mansion looms ahead, its towering spires silhouetted against the darkening sky. Torchlight flickers across its imposing stone walls.

Wrought iron gates worked with Valkaran and Asterian symbols stand open to the evening’s elite. Uniformed servants guide carriages through the cobblestone courtyard, and guests in lavish costumes and masks climb the grand staircase to the entrance.

At the foot of the staircase stand two guards in polished black armor bearing the Blackthorn family crest. Their hands rest casually on their sword hilts, but their eyes study every guest.

{actor: Barok Redbeard} One of the guards steps forward, a burly man with a red beard and a familiar, imposing frame.

{actor: Barok Redbeard} It is Barok, though far more composed now, his unruly beard neatly trimmed and his posture straighter, as if the formal attire demands it. His eyes narrow in recognition, but he says nothing of their last meeting. He raises a gloved hand.

{speaker: Barok Redbeard, delivery: restrained grunt} Invitation.

{actor: Barok Redbeard} His voice is as gruff as they remember. His eyes linger on each of them a moment longer than necessary.

## GM Notes

This guard is the same man they encountered during the Harvest Festival brawl—Barok, the rough-edged leader who had been quick to escalate tensions between rival Valkaran clans. 

**Disguises and Entry:**
The adventurers are expected to be wearing masks. If they chose costumes that contrast with their typical adventuring gear, they may gain a bonus to any Deception checks to remain anonymous. 

**Without an invitation.** Adventurers who turned down or lost Lady Astrid's invitation have none, since only she hands them out, in An Invitation. They can still get in: talk or bluff their way past Barok as guests whose invitation was lost or sent ahead, pay him off, join a noble party going up the stairs, or slip in with the servants and musicians at the side door. Whichever way they choose takes one fitting check for the whole party, and a failure costs a delay or a closer look, not the ball.

If the adventurers previously sided with Barok, he’ll be more accommodating and might allow them in with minimal scrutiny. If they opposed him, Barok will be unfriendly, scrutinizing their invitations and potentially causing a delay unless persuaded or intimidated. In a neutral scenario, Barok remains professional, checking their invites efficiently but becoming confrontational if disrespected.

**One check for the party.** Barok checks the party as one group, not hero by hero. An invitation covers everyone in the party. Without one, the hero who does the talking makes one check, and it covers them all. A failure costs one closer look and one more try, then Barok waves them up anyway with a warning: the Blackthorns want the hall full. Do not roll for each hero in turn.

**The door guard never speaks.** He answers with a nod, a look or a hand on his hilt, and leaves all talk to Barok.
