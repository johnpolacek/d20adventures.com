## Intro

Thaddeus Blackthorn’s private chambers are a stark contrast to the opulence of the rest of the mansion. The room is grand but cold: no lavish decorations or personal touches, just polished dark wood and expensive furnishings, meticulously arranged.

At its center stands an imposing mahogany desk, covered in neat stacks of papers, ledgers, and maps. Behind it, a large window looks out over moonlit Kordavos, its thick velvet curtains half-drawn, as if to limit how much can be seen.

The shelves along the walls hold no books, only scrolls and sealed letters, many bearing the insignias of prominent families, including the Blackthorn and Asterian crests. A glass cabinet in one corner holds rare, valuable trinkets: gems, ornate daggers, and a few objects that seem out of place, among them a small carved wooden idol of Valkaran origin.

On one wall hangs an ornate sword, its scabbard adorned with intricate Valkaran symbols. Its hilt gleams in the firelight, as if it means more than decoration. A second door, almost hidden behind a heavy tapestry, seems to lead to a smaller, more private study.

This is where business is done: deals struck, secrets kept, plans formed. It is easy to imagine Thaddeus behind the desk, plotting his further rise in Kordavos’s politics.

## GM Notes

The ornate long sword is lightly enchanted to slightly increase accuracy and damage. A hero may take it. The small study behind the tapestry holds a narrow cot, a washstand and a shelf of Asterian law books, nothing more.

**One party.** The heroes move as one group, and each room is played for all of them. If a hero goes off alone, a passing servant or a locked door turns them back, and they rejoin the others before the next room, having found nothing alone.

**The song.** Aelindor's song does not wait. At the end of the third round here, if the party has not left, the last note fades below, applause swells, and Thaddeus takes the stage. The party hurries back down to the ballroom and slips into the crowd as he begins. If the party has already searched two rooms since the song began, it ends at the end of the first round here instead.

**Caught.** If the house guard has the adventurers in hand, the guards do not march them about the house or search it with them. They take them straight down to the ballroom to answer to Lady Astrid, and arrive just as Thaddeus takes the stage. Every eye turns to him, and the guards hold the adventurers at the edge of the crowd while he speaks.

### The Desk

The imposing desk in the center of the room is immaculately organized, with a few unremarkable ledgers and papers neatly arranged. However, a small but intricate lock built into one of the desk drawers hints at something more valuable hidden within.

Upon closer inspection, it’s clear that the lock has been custom-designed, crafted by a master locksmith—perhaps even with arcane assistance. The keyhole is lined with faint engravings, barely visible to the untrained eye, and small runes subtly glow when tampered with. This mechanism is a blend of mechanical precision and magical security.

The lock on Thaddeus's desk is no ordinary mechanism. Detect Magic, or an Arcana check of DC 12, shows the ward. Disarming it takes thieves' tools or Dispel Magic, with a DC 15 check. Forcing or picking the drawer without disarming the ward triggers it, and so does a failed disarm. If the trap is triggered, everyone at the lock makes a DC 13 Dexterity saving throw:

Without warning, an arc of blue lightning crackles and surges from the metal components, leaping out toward anyone standing near. The room is briefly illuminated by an arcane electrical discharge, casting long, jagged shadows on the walls.

A failed save is a painful burn, a success only a singe. A protective spell cast before the lock is touched, such as Shield of Faith, gives the warded hero +2 on the save. No one is knocked out or carried off. The crack of the discharge carries, and the house guard from the corridor comes at the end of the next round unless the party has gone.

If they manage to bypass the lock and trap, either through skilled hands or magic, the drawer slides open to reveal a single, finely bound ledger. The ledger’s cover is made of black leather, and inside, the entries are written in neat, flowing script, detailing secretive transactions between the Blackthorn family and the elves of the Valkarr Forest.

The records are precise, listing dates, shipments, and the exchange of valuable artifacts—ancient relics from Old Valkara, including weapons, trinkets, and objects of mysterious power. 

Among the listed items, one particular entry stands out: "The Key of Ilmarin, acquired through negotiation, to be delivered in secret."

A later entry reads: "The Oath Stone of the First Clans, bought of the Circle of Faelendar at the Mordava pier. Proven by an Asterian arcanist." Beside it, in a different hand: "No loose ends. S." If the story so far says the crate was lost at the pier, that note reads instead: "Lost at the pier. Find it."
