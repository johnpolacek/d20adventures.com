## GM Notes

The characters may have expected this to be the Key, but it is instead a different relic. The Key is already in the possession of the Covenant of the Cowl.

Legacy stages:

### The First Bid

As the auctioneer's voice rings out, starting the bidding for the map, a tense silence falls over the room. Eyes dart nervously, and hands hesitate, knowing well the reputation of those who covet such rare treasures. 

From the back of the crowd, a figure steps forward—Silverhand’s representative, a tall man dressed in a sharp black coat. He raises a gloved hand calmly. “Ten thousand gold,” he states with an air of finality.

Seated behind him sits an undeniable presence exuding an air of cold calculation. His tall, slender frame is cloaked in a dark, richly embroidered coat. His left hand, encased in an intricate gauntlet of polished silver, rests on the arm of his chair. Beneath his hood, only the lower half of his face is visible, showing a thin-lipped smirk that hints at both intelligence and ruthlessness. Though his face remains shadowed, everyone in the room knows who he is: the man who controls the flow of illicit goods and information in Kordavos.

A murmur ripples through the crowd, but no one dares to speak. The weight of the bid—and more importantly, the man making it—casts a heavy shadow over the room. All know that to challenge Silverhand, even through his emissary, could mean far more than losing gold. The auctioneer pauses, scanning the room, but no one else dares lift their voice in opposition.

"Do I hear another bid?" the auctioneer asks tentatively, his voice cracking slightly in the tense atmosphere. Silence. "Ten thousand going once… going twice…"

**The bidding.** These numbers do not change.
- The auctioneer opens at five thousand gold. No one in the crowd bids.
- Silverhand's representative bids ten thousand.
- Any bid from anyone else, he tops at once by five thousand: fifteen, twenty, and so on. Silverhand always wins the map.
- The standing bid is the last one called. It never falls back to an earlier figure.
- The heroes carry no such sums. A purse holds tens of gold, not thousands, and no hero has a pouch or note worth thousands. A hero who bids must show the coin, and the auctioneer refuses a bid they cannot show. A bluffed bid only makes the representative top it.

**The map.** Neither the map nor its case carries any magic. Detect Magic finds nothing on them. The case stays shut until the lot is paid for. The auctioneer refuses any call to open it or let the room inspect it, and no hero sees more than the faded forest trails glimpsed at the unveiling.

**Objections.** The house names no sources and no buyers. A hero who calls the map stolen, or demands the sale stop, gets dry laughter from the room and the auctioneer's "Sit, or bid." The sale goes on.

**A grab.** The map case is chained to the lectern by a fine silver chain, and only the auctioneer holds the key. Six house guards with crossbows stand along the walls. A hero who seizes the case, by spell or by rushing the stage, holds it but cannot take it. The guards level their crossbows, and the auctioneer says once: "The house keeps its lots until they are paid for." A hero who lets go is escorted back to a seat. One who fights is clubbed senseless by the guards and comes to on the floor at the back of the hall as the gavel falls. Silverhand watches, says nothing, and marks the heroes' faces.

**The close.** Once no one else can top the bid, the auctioneer calls going once, going twice, and sold. Close the scene at the end of the second round at the latest.
