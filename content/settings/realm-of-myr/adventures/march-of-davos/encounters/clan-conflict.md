## Intro

The festival is in full swing with music and dance. On its outskirts, two groups stare each other down, and murmurs spread as the crowd senses the hostility.

{speaker: Barok Redbeard, to: Larya Halloway, delivery: shouting} Your people have dishonored us for the last time!

{actor: Barok Redbeard, target: Larya Halloway} His hand reaches for his sword.

{actor: Larya Halloway, target: Barok Redbeard} The other leader is a tall woman with a scar across her cheek.

{speaker: Larya Halloway, to: Barok Redbeard, delivery: snarling in response} And we won't tolerate your insults any longer!

{actor: Barok Redbeard, target: Larya Halloway} The first leader, a burly man with a red-dyed beard, steps forward. His followers move with him, rough sellswords in mismatched armor with Blackthorn ribbons tied at their sleeves. Behind them, an Asterian banner hangs over the Valkaran harvest altar at the square's edge. His eyes lock onto the scarred woman.

{speaker: Barok Redbeard, to: Larya Halloway, delivery: growling} We've been patient, but your arrogance knows no bounds, Larya.

Larya's people hold their ground, Valtor elders and their kin in old Valkaran festival dress, heirloom silver at their throats.

{speaker: Larya Halloway, to: Barok Redbeard, delivery: steady but filled with venom} It's you and your thugs who bring chaos wherever you go, Barok.

Onlookers inch away, leaving a circle around the two sides. Vendors glance about nervously, and parents pull their children close.

## GM Notes

The Valtor Clan, led by Larya, views the Blackthorn Clan member Barok and his followers as disrespectful intruders threatening their traditional way of life. Barok's people hung an Asterian banner over the Valkaran harvest altar for the Asterian parade, and the Valtors mean to tear it down.

Barok and his group have embraced the Asterian way of life and the Blackthorns pay them. They see the Valtor clan as archaic and obstructive. They have gained power and wealth since the Asterians' arrival and plan to keep doing so.

**The two sides.** Barok's crew are Blackthorn hired swords, so they wear patched, mismatched armor. Larya's people are an old Valkaran family, so they wear heirloom festival dress and silver. The traditionalists look richer than the newcomers.

**What each leader wants.**
- **Larya:** the Asterian banner off the harvest altar, and Barok's crew out of the square before the evening bells.
- **Barok:** the banner stays up for the parade, and Larya takes back "thugs" in front of the crowd.
- **A compromise both accept:** the banner moves from the altar to the parade stand across the square, Barok's crew leaves the altar to the Valtors, and neither leader apologizes. Other fair offers can work at the same DCs.

**Checks.** One leader at a time. Each needs one success to stand down.
- Persuasion with the compromise or another fair offer: DC 13. Without one: DC 16.
- Intimidation: DC 14 for Barok, who fears losing face before his crew. DC 16 for Larya, who keeps a cold head.
- A bluff that the watch is near: Deception DC 14 for Barok, DC 16 for Larya. It works like Intimidation.
- Insight DC 12 shows what each fears: Barok, looking weak before his crew. Larya, the Valtors shamed at their own Festival.
- A check that fails by 5 or more pushes that side a step toward blows. Swords are drawn, then the first blow falls.
- A spell or trick that holds the two sides apart counts as a success against the side it stops, with no check.
- Once blows fall, a hero may pull or trip one brawler out of the fight: Athletics or Acrobatics DC 13. It stops that one brawler and no more. Only the watch or both leaders standing down ends a brawl.
- A success stands down only the leader it was aimed at. The other stands down with it only if the winning offer already gives them all they asked for.
- Both leaders standing down ends the quarrel. They lead their people off into the crowd in a sentence or two.

**The watch.** If the quarrel is not settled by the end of the third round, or the first blow lands sooner, a squad of the city watch pushes in from the gate street with cudgels at the end of that round. Both clans scatter into the crowd. The watch checks the company against the gate book and lets them go with a warning. Tell it in a sentence or two. A hero who sends for the watch, or blows a whistle for it, brings it at the end of the next round. A fight before then is a brawl of fists and pommels. No one is killed.

**Favor.** Whatever the heroes did, each leader remembers it. A hero who sided with one side gains that clan's favor and the other's grudge. A fair settlement earns grudging respect from both.
