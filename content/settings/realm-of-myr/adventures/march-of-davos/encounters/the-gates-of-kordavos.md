## Intro

{place: gateArch} The adventurers approach the grand gates of Kordavos, towering stone archways hung with Asterian and Valkaran banners that flutter in the breeze. Laughter, music and chatter carry from the Harvest Festival just beyond.

{place: ledger} Before they can join the revelry, they must wait in a long line of travelers, merchants and villagers. It snakes back from a table beneath the arch, where a burly sergeant questions each traveler in turn, and it has barely moved in an hour.

{place: sergeant} The sergeant stands and bellows down the line: "Festival law! No one passes this gate alone. Travelers enter as a company, every name written in the gate book together, and each of you answers for the others while you're in the city."

{place: lineBack} Grumbling runs down the line. Families and merchant trains close ranks around their own. Those who came alone are left standing near one another, a few places back from the table, sizing one another up.

{hero: branka-stoneveil} A broad dwarf with copper braids and a twice-broken nose stares up at the old gate-ward sigil carved over the arch. The same sigil is stamped on her battered breastplate, and two of the guards have noticed.

{hero: cassia-verane} A precise Asterian woman in a grey coat lined with map pockets has a brass surveyor's glass trained on the gatehouse footings. She sketches in a thick folio, frowning as if the stones are older than they should be.

{hero: yeva-softstep} A small halfling in a festival cap has chatted her way three places up the line. She is cheerful with everyone, and her quick hazel eyes never stop counting the guards.

{hero: milos-radan} A grey-bearded priest in undyed robes, the bronze compass-star of the Church of Valkara at his chest, shares water from his gourd with a weary pilgrim family in the line ahead.

{hero: wrenna-faelendar} A tall elf in grey-green leathers carries a bundle of furs like a trader, yet her pale yew longbow is strung, and she watches the guards the way a scout watches a treeline.

{hero: ilya-veles} A half-elf in an embroidered festival coat plucks a few bars on the gusle across his back, and half the line hums along. His grey eyes move from face to face, as if each stranger were a verse.

{hero: thalbern} A lean, weathered man in a cloak of forest hues stands a little apart, longbow across his back. River mud still cakes his boots, and he keeps glancing back down the road toward the Mordava.

{hero: 1749159962941} A young scholar in spectacles keeps her hood up. The hem of her robe is stained with river water, and she peels apart the swollen pages of a book, looking up sharply whenever a guard passes.

{hero: 1749307435667} A halfling in a patchwork waistcoat works the line for gossip, trading a joke for every rumor, his bright hazel eyes darting toward the city as if he has lost something in it.

{place: ledger} Up at the table, a guard turns back a family three marks short of the fee. A string of covered wagons in Blackthorn colors rolls past the whole line, and the guards wave it through the gate without a glance. Behind the strangers, a spice trader fans himself and tells no one in particular that he has stood here since dawn.

The line will not move again for a while. There is nothing to do but wait, and the only company to be had is each other.

## GM Notes

This scene is where the heroes meet, before anyone at the gate asks them anything. It lasts one round. Each hero takes one turn in the line: to size up the others, strike up talk, ask about something they see, or offer to stand together. Then the line moves up to the sergeant's table. Give the heroes room to talk to each other. No one in this scene is in charge of them.

**Festival law.** Last year's Festival ended in a brawl between the clans, so the governor revived an old Valkaran custom of surety. During the Festival no traveler enters alone. Travelers enter as a company, their names written together in the gate book, and each answers to the watch for what the others do in the city. Families and merchant trains already count as companies. The heroes each came alone, so they must choose each other or wait outside until the Festival ends. The Stoneveils kept this custom when they were the gate-wards, and Branka knows it as her great-grandfather's law.

**The table is out of reach.** The sergeant at the table is Garlan Ironfist. He is too far up the line to hear the heroes or be heard, and calls no one up in this scene. Guards keep the line in order and send back anyone who tries to go ahead of it. A hero's business with the gate waits for the table.

**Between the heroes.** Each hero shows something the others can notice and ask about: Branka's sigil, Cassia's charts, Yeva's counting eyes, Milos's compass-star, Wrenna's strung bow, Ilya's song, Thalbern's river mud, Lyra's soaked books, Poppen's questions. When one hero asks another about it, leave the answer to that hero. Never answer for them. A hero with no paragraph in the intro is shown the first time the story turns to them, with one detail of how they look.

**Things in the line to talk about.**
- The family turned back three marks short. A hero may pay for them, and the family thanks them as it passes.
- The Blackthorn wagons waved through without inspection, while everyone else waits.
- The spice trader behind them, who has stood here since dawn and has opinions about the watch.
- A guard pulling a nervous traveler out of the line to search his pack.

The people in the line have no character sheets. Tell what they do and say in narration.

**Names.** No hero knows another's name. A hero may give theirs, ask for another's, or keep it. Whatever is said aloud is heard by all the heroes, who stand together.

**A hero who keeps to themselves.** Describe what the others can see of them. Do not force them to speak. Another hero may come to them.

**A hero who wants to go alone.** They may stay in the line. The law will catch up with them at the table.

## Transitions
