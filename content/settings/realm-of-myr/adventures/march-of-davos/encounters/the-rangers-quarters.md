## Intro

On the upper floors, the masquerade ball below is a distant echo. Wood creaks, and gusts of wind rattle the ornate windows.

At the end of a long corridor lined with Blackthorn family portraits is a heavy door, intricately carved with twisted branches and forest creatures, symbols of the Blackthorn clan’s tie to the Valkarr Forest.

## GM Notes

The door is locked. Thieves' tools open it with a DC 13 Dexterity check, or a DC 15 Strength check forces it, the crack lost under the music from below. A failed attempt costs time and nothing more. No one else is in this wing during the ball, and no guard comes up while the party works the door. The quarters are empty: no one hides there, and no one flees.

**One party.** The heroes move as one group, and each room is played for all of them. If a hero goes off alone, a passing servant or a locked door turns them back, and they rejoin the others before the next room, having found nothing alone.

**The song.** Aelindor's song does not wait. At the end of the third round here, if the party has not left, the last note fades below, applause swells, and Thaddeus takes the stage. The party hurries back down to the ballroom and slips into the crowd as he begins. If the party has already searched two rooms since the song began, it ends at the end of the first round here instead.

**Caught.** If the house guard has the adventurers in hand, the guards do not march them about the house or search it with them. They take them straight down to the ballroom to answer to Lady Astrid, and arrive just as Thaddeus takes the stage. Every eye turns to him, and the guards hold the adventurers at the edge of the crowd while he speaks.

Legacy stages:

### Inside Astrid’s Quarters

Inside, the room exudes a rustic elegance, a blend of Blackthorn wealth and her deep connection to the wilderness. Animal pelts line the floor, while the walls are decorated with mounted hunting trophies and maps of the Valkarr Forest. A large wooden desk stands near the window, scattered with papers, maps, and various trinkets—a reflection of Astrid’s meticulous planning and her ties to the Valkaran wilderness.

A smaller door in the back leads to a private study, where the faint scent of old leather and ink lingers in the air. On the desk, a few open books rest alongside a locked chest. A hunting bow is propped against the wall, and on the shelves, there are tomes and scrolls detailing elven lore and forest creatures.

A DC 14 Investigation or Perception check in the study finds the hidden compartment. The locked chest opens with thieves' tools at DC 12 and holds spare bowstrings, a whetstone and a purse of 20 gold, nothing of the Key.

### The Hidden Compartment

As the adventurers explore Astrid’s private study, they notice a faint draft coming from one of the bookcases, as if air is slipping through the cracks. Upon closer inspection, they find a small mechanism hidden within the intricate carvings along the side of the shelf. With a click, the bookshelf swings open, revealing a narrow compartment behind it.

**Nothing lies beyond the compartment.** It is a shallow niche in the shelf, and the draft comes down an old chimney flue no wider than a hand. No stair, passage or door opens from the study, and the Covenant's tunnels are not found from here.

Inside the hidden compartment is a worn, leather-bound journal. Its edges are frayed, and the pages are filled with the meticulous handwriting of a scholar. The cover bears the initials "M.A." 

The pages reveal sketches, historical notes, and cryptic clues tied to forgotten relics. It's now clear that Thaddeus ordered Mikhail's death and Astrid carried it out. She took this journal and hid it, even from her brother, to keep its knowledge for herself.

This is the very journal that Joran had been desperately searching for, believed lost when his father was murdered. The journal contains Mikhail Antonov’s research on ancient Valkaran artifacts, most notably his detailed investigation into the Key of Ilmarin and its significance to Valkaran history.

This journal represents both the culmination of Mikhail’s life’s work and the key to unraveling the truth behind his death—a truth the adventurers now hold in their hands.
